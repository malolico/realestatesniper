# RealEstateSniper Black Box — Retention and Evidence Policy

Conceptual policy for how the Black Box **preserves records, retains evidence, and governs availability** over time.

This document defines **retention philosophy, evidence custody, legal hold thinking, and archival discipline**. It does not define storage technology, deletion jobs, permissions implementation, or operational tooling.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Retention and Evidence Policy exists so that institutional memory remains **defensible long after the moment has passed** without becoming uncontrolled hoarding of sensitive material.

Its purpose is to answer:

- How long must accountability memory remain available, and under what conditions?
- How is evidence preserved, linked, and handled without replacing the original assertion?
- What happens when law, privacy, or business need requires restriction rather than exposure?
- How does the platform preserve history while respecting proportionality and lawful limits?

Retention is not infinite by default. Evidence is not optional decoration. Both must be governed deliberately.

---

## 2. Evidence Preservation Philosophy

Evidence turns assertion into **defensibility**. Preservation philosophy treats evidence as linked corroboration with its own custody obligations, not as a duplicate of the event itself.

Evidence preservation rests on five commitments:

1. **Assertion first** — The core event is recorded even when proof arrives later.
2. **Linkage over embedding** — Prefer governed reference and redaction to reckless inclusion of sensitive content in memory.
3. **Explicit custody** — Evidence must remain traceable from origin through review, restriction, export, and archival handling.
4. **Visible absence** — Missing, disputed, or withheld evidence must be representable without pretending certainty.
5. **Proportionality** — Preserve what dispute, audit, and lawful accountability require — not everything that can be collected.

The Black Box preserves proof paths, not evidence warehouses without purpose.

## 3. Record Retention Principles

Record retention governs **how long accepted event assertions remain available** under active policy.

**Necessity-driven retention**  
Retention duration follows legal, security, business, and dispute necessity — not dashboard convenience or storage habit.

**Category-aware thinking**  
Different event categories may warrant different retention posture because their dispute half-life and legal sensitivity differ. All category decisions must be explicit and reviewable.

**Governed availability**  
Retention changes availability and access posture; they do not rewrite historical assertions.

**Restriction before erasure**  
When limitation is required, governed access restriction and anonymization records are preferred over silent destruction of audit lineage unless law explicitly mandates otherwise.

**Retention is not immutability cancellation**  
An event may become archived or restricted while its original assertion remains intact and discoverable under authorized scope.

**Documented policy changes**  
When retention posture changes, the change itself must be reviewable and, where appropriate, recorded as a governance-visible fact.

Record retention protects investigability without promising eternal exposure of every assertion to every role.

---

## 4. Evidence Retention Principles

Evidence retention governs **how long corroborating material and its links remain available**.

**Linked retention**  
Evidence retention aligns with the events and cases it supports. Orphaned evidence without investigable linkage is a policy failure.

**Sensitivity escalation**  
Documents, identity material, financial artifacts, and privileged-action proof receive heightened retention and restriction care.

**Reference durability**  
When evidence lives outside core memory, the link and custody path must remain durable for as long as the supported assertion requires defensibility.

**Separate lifecycle**  
Evidence may arrive long after the event. Its retention clock may differ from the assertion clock, but the relationship must remain explicit.

**Disputed evidence remains visible**  
Contradictory or challenged proof is retained and qualified, not removed to simplify narrative.

**Minimum necessary preservation**  
Preserve enough to reconstruct what was known and when — not every incidental copy created along the way.

Evidence retention serves probative value, not archival appetite.

---

## 5. Chain of Custody Principles

Chain of custody is the **reconstructible path** by which evidence enters memory, is accessed, enriched, restricted, exported, and archived.

Custody principles:

- Origin of evidence must remain identifiable.
- Linkage to supported events must remain explicit.
- Access or export that materially affects custody must be governable and traceable where policy requires.
- Handling that changes trustworthiness must be visible, not silent.
- Copies outside governed export paths weaken custody and are discouraged.
- Custody must survive handoffs between investigation, legal review, and external disclosure.

Chain of custody matters most when proof must hold up outside the product: counsel, auditors, regulators, or formal dispute forums.

---

## 6. Evidence Integrity

Evidence integrity ensures that **linked proof remains authentic, complete enough for its class, and uncorrupted by handling**.

Integrity principles:

- Evidence must not silently replace or overwrite the assertion it supports.
- Enrichment based on evidence must be additive and trust-visible.
- Tampering, unauthorized substitution, or broken linkage must be detectable in principle.
- Recovery of lost evidence must be marked as reconstruction, not passed off as original custody.
- Integrity failures downgrade defensibility visibly rather than hiding behind confident language.

An event with corrupted evidence handling may remain historically true as an assertion while losing probative strength. That distinction must remain visible.

---

## 7. Evidence Availability

Evidence availability governs **who may access corroborating material, when, and for what purpose**.

Availability principles:

- Evidence access follows the same purpose-bound, scope-minimized logic as event access.
- Highly sensitive artifacts default to narrower visibility than the assertions they support.
- Redaction and reference may limit active availability while preserving custody metadata.
- Export packages include only evidence necessary to the scoped purpose.
- Unavailable evidence must be distinguishable from evidence that never existed.

Availability is not the same as retention. Material may be retained yet restricted. Investigators must see both facts clearly.

---

## 8. Investigation Retention

Investigation retention governs **how long investigation context remains available** after a case matures.

Investigation principles:

- Case scope, review activity, and export history may themselves require retention when they affect accountability or legal posture.
- Investigation material must not become an informal long-term archive outside governed policy.
- Closed investigations may move to reduced active availability without erasing that review occurred.
- Cross-case linkage must remain possible when later disputes reopen related truth.
- Investigation retention aligns with legal, security, and business need — not with personal convenience of reviewers.

Investigation retention preserves the memory of how truth was examined, not endless operational clutter.

---

## 9. Legal Hold Philosophy

Legal hold is the **governed suspension of ordinary retention or restriction changes** when formal legal or regulatory need requires preservation.

Legal hold principles:

- Hold applies to identified scope: actors, cases, categories, time windows, or linked evidence — not vague platform-wide freezes without accountability.
- Hold initiation, scope change, and release must be attributable and reviewable.
- Ordinary disposal or restriction processes must not proceed in ways that undermine active hold scope.
- Hold does not authorize rewriting assertions; it preserves availability for lawful need.
- Release from hold returns material to governed ordinary retention posture with visible decision.

Legal hold protects defensibility under challenge. It is not a permanent state and not a substitute for case discipline.

---

## 10. Historical Preservation

Historical preservation ensures that **accepted institutional memory remains intelligible across time**, even as product state and vocabulary evolve.

Preservation principles:

- Original assertions remain addressable under the meaning they held when accepted.
- Taxonomy and naming evolution must not retroactively relabel past truth.
- Additive explanation may clarify older material; it may not erase it.
- Recovery, provisional status, and dispute must remain visible in historical reconstruction.
- Preservation prefers lineage continuity over cosmetic cleanliness.

The platform may change forward. Historical preservation ensures the Black Box still remembers what was true then.

---

## 11. Archival Philosophy

Archival is the **transition from active investigation availability to long-term governed preservation**.

Archival principles:

- Archived material remains part of institutional memory; archiving is not deletion by another name.
- Archival changes expected immediacy of access, not historical existence.
- Correlation, lineage, and legal relevance must survive archival transition.
- Archival posture may differ by category sensitivity and retention class.
- Retrieval under authorized need must remain possible within governed bounds.

Archiving manages temperature, not truth. Cold memory must still be honest memory.

---

## 12. Evidence Disposal Principles

Evidence disposal governs **when corroborating material or its active availability may end** under lawful and reviewed policy.

Disposal principles:

- Disposal is a governed decision, not silent expiry.
- Prefer restriction, redaction, or reference retirement before destructive removal where policy allows.
- Disposal of evidence must consider linked events, active holds, and ongoing investigations.
- When destruction is legally required, lineage records should explain what was removed and why unless law forbids even that.
- Disposal must not create the appearance that the original assertion never happened.

Disposal reduces exposure and cost; it must not launder accountability.

---

## 13. Compliance Principles

Compliance aligns retention and evidence handling with **lawful, proportionate, and purpose-bound** conduct.

Compliance principles:

- Collection and retention require identifiable lawful purpose and proportionality to accountability needs.
- Privacy rights and jurisdictional limits are first-class constraints, not afterthoughts at export time.
- Regulator, partner, and formal user dispute responses use scoped packages with traceable authorization.
- Consent-relevant and access-relevant memory receives category-appropriate retention thinking.
- Compliance prefers governed restriction and anonymization records over untracked erasure when law permits.

Compliance makes memory defensible to outsiders, not merely useful to insiders.

---

## 14. Governance of Retention

Retention and evidence policy is **governed, not improvised** by individual teams or urgent moments.

Governance principles:

- Material retention or evidence policy changes require reviewed approval with security and legal participation when sensitivity demands.
- Category retention posture is owned at platform level, not by local emitter convenience.
- Hold, disposal, archival, and restriction actions must be attributable.
- Policy exceptions are temporary, scoped, and documented — not normalized by habit.
- Retrospective review evaluates whether preservation remained investigable without overexposure.

Governance ensures retention policy outlives staffing changes and executive pressure.

---

## 15. Future Evolution Rules

Retention and evidence policy may evolve only when evolution **strengthens truth, trust, defensibility, or lawful proportionality**.

**Allowed evolution**

- Clearer category retention guidance as dispute patterns mature
- Stronger custody and availability rules for document-heavy cases
- Better articulation of hold, archival, and restriction interaction
- Improved visibility of missing, provisional, or reconstructed evidence
- Tighter proportionality rules when collection risk grows

**Evolution requirements**

Every proposed change must answer:

1. Which retention or evidence risk does this address?
2. Does it preserve historical assertions and lineage?
3. Does it reduce reckless exposure or ungoverned copying?
4. Does it remain compatible with legal hold and compliance constraints?
5. Can older material remain investigable under the new policy without silent reinterpretation?

**Disallowed evolution**

- Indefinite hoarding without purpose because storage is cheap
- Silent deletion to avoid embarrassment or commercial inconvenience
- Retention rules driven by dashboard cleanup rather than accountability need
- Evidence practices that conflate proof with assertion
- Policy exceptions that become undeclared permanent norms

Amendments to this policy require explicit review alongside the constitution and governance corpus. Convenience is not sufficient justification.

Retention and evidence mature forward. Past truth must not be traded for present comfort.

---

## Closing Note

The Retention and Evidence Policy defines **how long memory lives, how proof is preserved, and how availability changes without rewriting truth**.

Evidence preservation and record retention serve defensibility under proportionate rules. Chain of custody, integrity, and availability keep proof trustworthy. Investigation retention, legal hold, historical preservation, and archival philosophy manage time without laundering the past. Disposal and compliance keep the platform lawful. Governance and future evolution keep policy deliberate as RealEstateSniper grows.

Operational mechanisms come later. This document defines the principles those mechanisms must serve.
